Field notes from Sunburst Road

What’s out there,
and how far

There has been a notebook by the front door of Mojave Ghost since we opened. Guests borrow it, add to it, occasionally argue with it in the margins. This is most of what’s in there, minus the coffee rings. Drive times are measured from our gate.

01

Three ways into the park

The West Entrance in the village of Joshua Tree is roughly 12 minutes from us and it is the one everybody uses. The North Entrance in Twentynine Palms takes about 25 minutes and the line is usually half as long. The South Entrance at Cottonwood, off the I-10, puts you in the low Colorado desert near the cholla garden.

  • Buy the pass online. Seven-day vehicle pass, printed or on your phone, saves the kiosk wait.
  • Leave by 7:30am on weekends. After 8:30 the West gate backs up past the fire station.
  • No fuel inside the park. Fill up in the village or in Twentynine Palms.
02

Trailheads we send people to

You can see the good parts of this park in a long morning. Carry more water than feels reasonable, a liter per person per hour once it warms up, and start early from May onward.

Ryan Mountain
3 miles, 1,050 feet up. Best view in the park.
Hidden Valley Loop
1 mile, flat, easy with small kids.
Barker Dam
1.3 miles. Petroglyphs near the far end.
49 Palms Oasis
3 miles round trip, steep, real fan palms.
Lost Horse Mine
4 miles. The stamp mill is still standing.
03

Where the sky actually goes black

You do not have to drive anywhere for this. Our deck faces east with no porch light on the whole property, and there are two reclining chairs stacked by the hot tub. The red flashlight in the kitchen drawer keeps your eyes adjusted.

  • Cap Rock lot inside the park, 25 minutes, flat horizon and easy parking.
  • Quail Springs picnic area for the widest view west.
  • Check the moon. New moon weekends between March and October give you the Milky Way core after about 10pm.
04

Roadside food, ranked by us

The high desert eats early and closes early. Two of these need reservations on a Saturday, and one of them is a 20 minute drive through country you will want to see in daylight anyway.

  • Crossroads Cafe, village breakfast, put your name down and walk the block.
  • La Copine out in Flamingo Heights. Closed Monday and Tuesday. Go at opening.
  • Pappy & Harriet’s in Pioneertown. Book a table or eat at the bar and listen to whoever is playing.
  • Natural Sisters for coffee before the park gate.
  • Village grocery to stock the cabin kitchen. There is nothing to buy past our turnoff.
05

Day trips, gate to gate

Save one of these for the day you do not feel like hiking. The Route 66 run and the Salton Sea are long, hot and completely worth the gas.

Pioneertown
25 minutes. An old western film set that never left.
The Integratron, Landers
35 minutes. Sound baths book out weeks ahead.
Amboy and Roy’s sign
About 1 hour 15. Fill the tank first.
Salton Sea, Bombay Beach
About 1 hour 45. Strange and quiet and worth it.
Palm Springs Tramway
1 hour. Thirty degrees cooler at the top in August.

The property

A homestead cabin that nobody was using

The original structure went up in the 1950s under the Small Tract Act, one room and a wood stove, built by somebody who wanted five acres and got them. It sat empty for a long stretch. We put a real roof on it, added the second bedroom, ran the water line and left the rest alone: the mesquite floorboards, the crooked window over the sink, the yucca that leans into the porch.

The name came from the guests. A jackrabbit hangs around the wash at dusk and turns up pale in the porch light, and enough people asked about the ghost that we stopped correcting them.

We live nearby and clean the place ourselves. If a hot tub jet is off or the propane runs low, you are texting the people who will fix it, not a call center.
