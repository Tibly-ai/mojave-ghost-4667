The Cabin

Two bedrooms, four made beds, and about a quarter mile to the nearest porch light.

The cabin started life as a 1954 homestead shack. It has grown a second bedroom, a real bathroom, and a deck that faces east so the sunrise wakes you before your phone does. Everything here is meant to be used: the cast iron, the record player, the wool blankets by the fire pit. Below is exactly what you get, written out plainly so you can decide before you drive four hours.

The specifics

01 Sleeping
Sleeps 4. Bedroom one has a king with a wool-topped mattress; bedroom two has two twins that push together into a king if you ask ahead. No sofa bed, no air mattresses, no surprise cots.
02 Bathroom
One full bath with a tiled walk-in shower, deep basin sink, and an outdoor rinse shower off the back wall for after the trails. Well water, softened, drinkable.
03 Kitchen
Full kitchen: gas range, full-size fridge, butcher block counter, drip coffee and a stovetop percolator, cast iron, sharp knives, and enough plates and glasses for six. Dishwasher, yes.
04 Hot tub
Four-person cedar-clad hot tub on the side deck, kept at 102°F year round and drained between every stay. Towels and a robe hook are right at the steps.
05 Pool
No pool on the property. The hot tub runs all year, and the county pool in town is open through summer.
06 Stargazing deck
A 20-foot east-facing deck with two reclining loungers, a folding star chart, and no fixed lighting overhead. Every exterior bulb is amber and on a switch you control.
07 Fire pit
Steel ring set in decomposed granite, four canvas chairs, and a first bundle of firewood on the house. Subject to county burn restrictions in high wind and late summer.
08 Workspace
A solid desk by the north window with a real chair, a power strip, and fiber internet averaging 180 Mbps. Cell service is spotty; the wifi calling works fine.
09 Pets
Two dogs welcome for a flat $40 per stay. The yard is fenced on three sides, so leash them for the open desert side. Please keep them off the beds.
10 Views
Open desert on all four sides, the Little San Bernardinos to the south, and Bortle 2 skies overhead. The Milky Way is visible with the naked eye from March into October.
11 Comfort
Central heat and air, a wood stove in the main room, ceiling fans in both bedrooms, blackout curtains, washer and dryer, and an iron nobody has ever asked for.
12 Arrival
Self check-in with a keypad code, parking for two vehicles on graded dirt, and the last 300 feet of driveway is washboard. Any sedan makes it slowly.

Since 2016

We bought a rockhound's shack and kept most of his ideas.

The man who homesteaded this parcel hauled water in a truck and set his kitchen window to face the sunrise. We rebuilt the roof, ran a well, added the second bedroom, and left that window exactly where he put it.

Five acres means the nearest neighbor is a shape on the horizon, not a sound. We live twelve minutes up the road, so if the propane runs low or you cannot find the star chart, we can be over before the coffee cools.

Nights here are dark enough that people stop talking for a while. That is the whole idea of Mojave Ghost.

Who it suits

Four people, three good reasons to drive out here.

Two people, no plans

Arrive Friday at four, get in the tub by six, cook something on the range, and let the fire burn down. Most couples never use the car again until checkout.

A quiet work week

Fiber internet, a desk with a view, and calls that nobody interrupts. Weekday stays from Sunday through Thursday have no two-night minimum, and the rate drops after five nights.

Small celebrations

Birthdays, anniversaries, an elopement with two witnesses. Tell us what you are marking and we will leave out the good glasses and a bundle of extra firewood. Four guests total, no events or day visitors.

How many people can actually sleep here? +

Four, and that is a hard cap on the county permit. A king in one room, two twins in the other. We do not allow extra guests, tents, or vehicles sleeping on the property.

Is the hot tub really open all year? +

Yes. It is drained, scrubbed, and refilled between every stay, which takes about six hours, so it may still be warming if you arrive right at four. January soaks at 40°F outside are the best ones.

What is the road like? +

Paved to within 300 feet of the gate, then graded dirt with a washboard stretch. Rental sedans get here fine at ten miles an hour. After heavy rain, take it slower and hug the right side.

Do you provide firewood and coffee? +

One bundle of firewood, a bag of local coffee, salt, pepper, oil, and the basics for a first breakfast. The market in town is nine minutes away and closes at nine.

How far is the park entrance? +

About fourteen minutes to the West Entrance station on Park Boulevard. Hidden Valley and Barker Dam are another twenty minutes past the gate. More trail notes live on the Joshua Tree page.

What does a night cost, and what is the minimum? +

Around $180 a night with a two-night minimum on Friday and Saturday. Cleaning and county taxes are shown before you confirm. Full breakdown on Rates & Booking.
